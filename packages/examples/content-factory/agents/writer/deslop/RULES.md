# Deslop rules (condensed)

Condensed from [skill-deslop](https://github.com/stephenturner/skill-deslop) by Stephen D. Turner, MIT License (see LICENSE in this folder). The full skill has longer catalogs and examples; this list keeps the rules a draft must pass.

`check.mjs` flags the mechanical ones. The rest need judgement: reread the draft against them.

## Words and phrases

1. No em dashes or en dashes. Use a comma, a period or parentheses.
2. No throat-clearing openers: "Here's the thing", "Here's why", "The truth is", "Let me be clear", "It turns out".
3. No emphasis crutches: "Let that sink in", "Full stop", "Make no mistake", "This matters because".
4. No hand-holding: "Let's dive in", "Let's break this down", "Think of it as", "Imagine a world where".
5. No business jargon. Use the plain word: use (not leverage or utilize), strong (not robust), simplify (not streamline), field (not landscape or ecosystem), examine (not delve or unpack).
6. No adverbs doing nothing: really, just, literally, genuinely, honestly, simply, actually, deeply, truly, fundamentally, quietly, importantly, crucially, notably.
7. No filler transitions: "It's worth noting", "At its core", "At the end of the day", "When it comes to", "In today's world", "In conclusion".
8. Say "is", not "serves as", "stands as" or "represents".

## Sentences

9. No binary contrasts ("It's not X, it's Y", "Not X. Y."). State Y.
10. No question you answer yourself in the next breath ("The result? Chaos.").
11. No stacks of short dramatic fragments. Mix sentence lengths; three sentences of the same length in a row is a smell.
12. Two items beat three. Avoid reflexive lists of three.
13. Active voice with a person as the subject. "We cut the discount", not "The discount was cut".
14. No inanimate thing doing a human verb ("the data tells us"). Name who learned it.

## Substance

15. Be specific: a number, a name of a thing, a date. Cut "the implications are significant" and say the implication.
16. No vague attribution ("experts say"). If you cannot name the source, cut the claim.
17. No invented concept labels ("the explanation paradox").
18. One point per post. Do not restate it three ways.
19. Do not end on a punchy one-liner or a signposted conclusion.

## Formatting

20. No bold-first bullets, no unicode arrows, no emoji walls. At most two hashtags, at the end, only on LinkedIn.
